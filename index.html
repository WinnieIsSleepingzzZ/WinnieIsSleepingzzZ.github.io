About

I am a doctoral student at the University of Tokyo, working on how consumer-grade unmanned aerial vehicles and deep learning can turn coastal and shallow-water environments into something you can actually measure — not just photograph.

Most environmental litter surveys report counts, because counts are what a human walking a transect can record. My work asks what becomes visible when a survey returns an outline for every individual object instead: how much of a beach a given class of debris really covers, which sources dominate by mass rather than by tally, and how cheaply that measurement can be repeated. The same question runs through my work on riverbeds, shallow reefs, and amphibious UAV platforms.

My doctoral work is supported by the Japan Science and Technology Agency through the SPRING Program (JPMJSP2108).

Research interests

  • UAV remote sensing for environmental monitoring — low-altitude survey design, orthomosaic workflows, centimetre- and sub-centimetre-scale ground sampling
  • Instance segmentation and lightweight vision models — per-object masks under heavy background mimicry, efficient architectures that run without a cluster
  • Marine and riverine litter quantification — area-based rather than count-based metrics, and what the two disagree about
  • Amphibious and aerial-aquatic platforms — super-resolution reconstruction and detection for shallow-water ecological survey
